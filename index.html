The Pattern I See

Growth makes everything
more complicated.

In my experience, companies rarely break because someone had a bad idea. They break because the way they work can't keep up with what they're trying to do. I help you figure out where that's happening, and fix it before it costs you.

  • 01
    Everyone is busy, but nothing moves faster

    People are working hard and the results are still flat. Usually the problem isn't the people. It's the system they're working inside.

  • 02
    Teams are pulling in different directions

    Sales, operations, and product are all moving, just not toward the same thing. Work gets done twice, or it falls through the cracks.

  • 03
    Processes built for 10 people break at 40

    What worked when you were small starts to drag once you grow. Growth finds every seam you stitched over.

  • 04
    You're adding AI before the operation is ready for it

    New tools get layered on top of workflows that were already broken. AI is very good at making things bigger, so it makes the mess bigger too.

My Approach

Growth & Operations Diagnostic

I start by looking at how your business actually runs day to day, which is usually not how anyone thinks it runs. Then I show you where the growth is leaking, get your team to agree on what to fix first, and rebuild the systems that have to carry you to the next stage.

AI is a big part of how I work, but I don't reach for it automatically. Where it takes real work off your team, I'll build it in. Where the judgment has to stay human, I'll leave it alone.

Workflow Analysis

I map your important processes from beginning to end, so we can both see where the time and the money are going.

Bottleneck Identification

We find the exact handoffs and decisions that are holding everything else up.

System Redesign

Then we rebuild what needs rebuilding: roles, tools, the way information moves, so your team can work at the pace you need.

AI Integration

Where AI genuinely saves your team time, I'll put it in and set it up properly. Automation, LLM workflows, the tooling around them. Only where it earns its place.
Selected Work

Some of the work.

Telecom · 2007–2010

TracFone Wireless

Systems Design Customer Retention Cross-functional

Rebuilt the self-service account system for a national phone carrier with millions of customers.

TracFone, then part of América Móvil and now part of Verizon, was losing customers for a reason that had nothing to do with price. People couldn't manage their own accounts, so they called support for everything, and some of them just left. I was the systems improvement engineer on the rebuild. I worked with the design, engineering, and QA teams in sprint cycles, and every cycle was tied to something specific that customers were struggling with.

Outcome: Fewer calls to support for things people could do themselves, across millions of active accounts, and teams that finally agreed on what they were measuring.

Automotive / LATAM · Contract · May–Oct 2007

Volkswagen

Multi-market Content CMS Migration Regional Coordination

Ran the content for twelve Latin American web platforms and helped move them onto Volkswagen Germany's central CMS.

Every Latin American market was running its own web content, so the brand looked different in every country and the same work was being done twelve times over. I was the eCommerce content manager on a six-month contract. I owned the content for those twelve platforms and the product brochures that went with them, and I helped move the sites onto Volkswagen Germany's in-house CMS. Retail / eCommerce · Founded 2011 · Repositioned 2022

Fetes de Lili

Founder · Self-Directed Market Repositioning Digital Operations

Rebuilt my own party supply business around a customer who had completely changed.

This one is mine. I founded it in 2011, and from 2012 to 2016 I tested an eco-friendly children's products line through Zulily and Groupon. After COVID the market moved underneath us. EdTech · 2020–2023

Founder · Self-Directed MVP Build Service Delivery

Designed how a live online robotics school would actually run, from class size to tool stack.

Playful Circuits had the idea and needed a way to deliver it that could repeat. Training & Enablement

New systems don't fail on the build. They fail on the handoff.

You can buy exactly the right software and still get nothing out of it. People keep working the way they always did, adoption stalls, and the money quietly does nothing.

I build the training that makes a new system stick, and I teach it myself. Most documentation gets written by the people who built the thing, which is why it explains the software instead of the job. I come at it from the other side. I've spent more than ten years teaching engineering, robotics, and programming to people seeing it for the first time, so I know how to explain something to someone who has never seen it before.

Live Delivery

Common Questions

Questions I get asked.

What do you actually do?

I'm an operations consultant. I work with growing companies in the United States, Spain, and Latin America, and I start by looking at how a business actually runs day to day, which is usually not what the org chart says. Then I find where the growth is leaking, rebuild the workflows and roles and tools that are holding things up, put AI in where it takes real work off the team, and train everyone on whatever we end up shipping.

What is a Growth & Operations Diagnostic?

It's four things. First I map your important processes from beginning to end, so we can both see where the time and the money are going. Then we find the exact handoffs and decisions that hold everything else up. Then we rebuild what needs rebuilding: roles, tools, the way information moves. And where AI genuinely saves your team time, I put it in and set it up properly. What you get at the end is an honest picture of how your operation works right now, and a list of what to fix first.

How long does an engagement take?

The diagnostic runs four to six weeks. Training work is scoped to whatever rollout it supports, so it can run alongside the diagnostic or come after it.

Do you work with companies outside Miami?

Yes. Most of my work is remote, with clients in the United States, Spain, and Latin America. I work in English, Spanish, or French, which matters more than people expect on multi-market rollouts, where one working language usually doesn't cover every team.

How do you decide where AI belongs in a business?

I don't reach for it automatically. The diagnostic comes first, so the decision gets made against how your business actually runs, instead of against a list of tools somebody is selling. Where AI takes real work off your team, I'll build it in: automation, LLM workflows, the tooling around them. Where the judgment has to stay human, I leave it alone. Putting AI on top of a broken workflow just makes the mess bigger.

Who delivers the training?

I do. Most software documentation gets written by the people who built the thing, which is why it explains the software instead of the job. I come at it from the other side. Alongside the operations work, I've spent more than ten years teaching engineering, robotics, and programming to people seeing it for the first time, in the United States, in France, and in Spain.

What industries have you worked in?

Telecom, with TracFone Wireless, which was part of América Móvil and is now part of Verizon. Automotive, across Volkswagen's Latin American web platforms. Retail and eCommerce, EdTech, B2B software, and K-12 education. What ties them together is that they were all complicated or regulated in some way, and several teams had to agree on one way of working before anything could move.

Want to find out where your operation is leaking?

It starts with one conversation. No deck, no jargon, just an honest look at how your operation works right now and where AI would actually help.
